A sewage backup is one of the most hazardous situations a property owner can face. Anyone who comes in contact with contaminated material is exposed to parasites, bacteria and viruses, among them giardia, E. coli, salmonella and hepatitis, all carried in raw sewage and all dangerous. Dallas Restoration Squad provides emergency sewage cleanup throughout Dallas, responding 24/7 with properly trained technicians, appropriate personal protective equipment and EPA-registered disinfectants to decontaminate your property safely.

Water damage comes in categories, and sewage sits in the worst one: Category 3, called black water, the highest level of contamination and danger. That rating calls for the strictest protocols: containment barriers go up, crews wear full protective equipment, every porous material the sewage touched is removed, and the surfaces that stay are disinfected thoroughly. Our technicians follow IICRC S500 standards for Category 3 water damage so your property is restored to safe occupancy standards.

We clear the standing sewage first, then pull contaminated water out of structural materials, remove carpet, padding, insulation, drywall and any other porous item that was in contact with sewage for proper disposal, disinfect each hard surface, treat with antimicrobial products and finish with structural drying, which prevents secondary mold. Lingering sewage odor is eliminated with thermal fogging and industrial hydroxyl generators, which we run in the affected area.

Why Sewage Cleanup Is Not a DIY Job

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Hepatitis, gastrointestinal disease and infections of the skin or respiratory system are among the serious illnesses these pathogens cause.

Professional sewage cleanup also requires proper disposal of contaminated materials as biohazard waste. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Licensed biohazard disposal facilities take this waste from certified restoration firms, which keep the required protocols in place.

Maintenance Tips for Sewage Cleanup

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages
  • Know the location and condition of your sewer cleanout for emergency access
  • Hair and other debris are easy to stop with simple strainers on tub, shower and sink drains
  • Wipes clog sewer lines even when the label says flushable, so only toilet paper goes down the toilet
  • Keep downspouts and sump pump discharge out of the sanitary sewer so it does not overload in heavy rain
  • Every 2 to 3 years, get a sewer camera run through the line to spot developing issues

Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Wet spots on floors or walls accompanied by foul odors
  • Sewage or dark water backing up through floor drains, toilets or tubs
  • Carpet or flooring contaminated when a toilet overflowed with solid waste
  • Lower levels or drain openings giving off a heavy sewage odor, sometimes like rotten eggs
  • A cleanout that weeps sewage, or wet foul patches in the lawn along the sewer path
  • Several fixtures backing up at once instead of only one

Our Sewage Cleanup Process

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle sewage cleanup from start to finish:

Hazard Check and Containment

We start by measuring how far the sewage contamination reaches, noting any safety hazards and setting up containment so clean areas are not cross-contaminated.

Common Sewage Cleanup Questions

The emergency phase, meaning sewage removal plus decontamination, generally lasts 1 to 3 days. Complete restoration, including material replacement, may take 1 to 3 weeks depending on the extent of damage. Our first priority is making the property safe to occupy again as fast as we can.
What each item is made of decides that. We can generally clean and disinfect non-porous belongings, for example glass, metal, hard plastic and tile that is sealed. Upholstery, mattresses, carpet with its pad, insulation and drywall soak up sewage and cannot be made safe, so they are thrown out. Each discarded item is documented, giving you a record to use with your insurer.
We typically recommend temporary relocation during sewage cleanup, especially if the contamination in your home is extensive. Both the decontamination chemicals and pathogens in the air can be a risk to your health. We will advise you based on the specific situation in your home.
Nobody, pets included, should walk through the area the sewage reached. Do not flush, run faucets or start the washer or dishwasher, because added water feeds the backup. Switching off the heating and cooling and the affected circuits is wise, provided you stay clear of the contaminated water while doing it. Leave the cleanup alone, and wash well with soap if any got on your skin.
Most backups start with a blockage in the home's sewer line. Roots from nearby trees, flushed wipes and accumulated grease account for a large share of those clogs. Other possibilities are a failed old pipe and a municipal sewer overloaded by heavy rain, which sends sewage up through floor drains. To know for certain, have the line camera-inspected once cleanup is finished.
