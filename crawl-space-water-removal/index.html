Crawl Space Water Removal Help in Dallas

Water under the house is easy to miss. Most people find it when the floors feel cold and damp, a musty smell comes up through the vents, or someone opens the access hatch and sees a pond. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Our team pumps the water out, clears what is ruined and dries the framing before it softens.

Getting the water out is only the first half. Wood framing takes water in gradually and gives it up just as gradually, which is why we meter the joists, beams and subfloor and leave dehumidifiers on until readings drop, typically 3 to 5 days. Our team traces the source too, whether that is a supply line leak, a drain, or rain driven groundwater, so the space stays dry afterward.

What It Looks Like

  • A look through the hatch shows pooled water or soupy soil
  • A musty, earthy smell that is strongest near the floor vents
  • Batts of insulation drooping or dangling from the underside of the floor
  • Edges of the floorboards lifting into a washboard pattern in the rooms above
  • Floors above that feel cold, damp, soft or slightly bouncy
  • Steel posts, hangers for pipes and duct straps showing orange rust
Our Process

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. After that our team marks out the wet area and records how much moisture the framing and subfloor hold.

2

Pump Out Water

We run pumps and extraction lines until the pooled water is gone, low pockets and trenches along the footings included. Mud and silt left on the liner are cleared at the same time.

3

Clear Out Ruined Material

Debris, saturated batts and any liner that cannot be reused leave the crawl space in bags. Our crew then wipes down the uncovered framing and applies antimicrobial to spots with growth or a high chance of it.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.

Shifting Piers and Supports

When the ground stays soaked, footings may lose support and piers can tilt or sink. Sloping floors or sticking doors in the rooms above may point to this.

Rot You Cannot See

Decay tends to begin along the upper edge of the framing or where it meets the sill, out of view from the access opening. Our crew tests the lumber by hand to locate weak areas.

Contaminated Water

What collects beneath a home is frequently dirty. A leaking drain line or groundwater carrying soil and waste is Category 2 or 3 and needs protective gear and disinfection.

If Nothing Is Done

How the Damage Progresses

First day

Water Pools Below

Low areas fill first while the ground underneath takes on water. Insulation starts wicking it up and humidity under the floor climbs fast.

24 to 48 hours

Early Mold Growth

Paper faced insulation and moist joists are where mold is able to appear first. Soaked insulation gains weight and starts to sag off the floor above.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. Floors may start to cup, rooms smell musty and the wood framing keeps absorbing water.

Past one week

Wood Starts Rotting

Wood that stays wet begins to decay. Sill plates and joists lose firmness, nails and hangers corrode, and termites along with other pests move toward the moisture.
